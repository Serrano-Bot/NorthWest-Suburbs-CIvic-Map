Print setup
Printing: set the title, paper size, and orientation above, then use "Print current view." The printed sheet gets its own header with a name/date line and an auto-built legend of whatever layers are turned on — handy for handing out the right answer key alongside the blank worksheets.
City/village layer: now filtered to only municipalities that actually intersect the HSD 211 boundary — so places like Barrington Hills, South Barrington, Arlington Heights, Rolling Meadows, Streamwood, Bartlett, and Bloomingdale/Addison drop out automatically, even though they're inside the map's visible area.
Colors: now assigned so no two touching polygons ever share a similar color — it's a graph-coloring pass over a 12-color palette, not a simple rotation.
Color fills: each polygon in a layer now gets its own distinct color, and the "Fill" slider under each layer controls how see-through it is over the roads. This is for telling regions apart on screen — when you print, everything still goes through the same grayscale filter as the streets, so colors come out as distinct shades of gray on the xerox, not color. Turn fill down to ~15–20% if you want the roads to stay legible underneath a busy layer like census tracts.
How to use this: Leave every layer off and print for a blank student worksheet — roads only. Turn on one layer at a time as the answer key for that exercise (township, then city, then school district, then tract). "HSD 211 boundary" and "HSD 211 schools + office" are separate from the general school-district layer above them — use those two for the Township High School District 211 unit specifically.
HS attendance boundaries: the toggle above uses a 2015–16 federal survey (NCES SABS) as a rough approximation — the best public data available, but not current. For the real, current boundaries, use the district's own official map instead: D211_official_boundary_map.pdf (keep it in the same folder as this file). It's in color and meant as your answer key / grading reference, not something to hand out blank.
School districts: now pulls both unified and secondary districts from a wider search area, so District 211 (secondary), District 214 (secondary, to the east), and Lake Park District 108 (secondary, to the south) all show up alongside the elementary/unified districts — not just whatever the original two-township view happened to catch.
County / hydrology: Counties is bbox-limited to Cook and everything that touches it (DuPage, Kane, Lake IL, McHenry, Will, Lake IN). Hydrology now pulls the Census's own local-detail hydrography — every named creek, pond, and small lake in that same bordering-county area, not just the major rivers.
Voting precincts: pulled from the Cook County Clerk's own precinct layer, filtered to just Schaumburg and Palatine Township (their Township_Name field, not a guess by geometry). With dozens of precincts in play, permanent labels would bury the map — click any precinct instead to see its name in a popup.
Polling places: real polling-place points from the Clerk's own election service (not precinct centroids), same township filter as the precinct layer.
Census tracts: tract numbers are hidden until you click a tract, to keep the map from being covered in GEOIDs at a glance.
D54: one combined layer — Schaumburg CCSD 54's approximate 2015–16 attendance zones (same NCES caveat as the HS boundaries above) plus a labeled point for all ~27 current school locations, fetched live from NCES's school geocode file.
Zoning: zoning isn't in federal Census data — it's set by each municipality. For a zoning base layer, pull from Schaumburg's map gallery or Palatine's village GIS site directly.
Boundary data: U.S. Census Bureau TIGERweb (live). Basemaps: OpenStreetMap contributors / Esri.
